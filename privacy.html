1. Introduction and Scope

MedAI is an AI-powered healthcare platform that helps people understand symptoms, receive AI-assisted triage, maintain an ongoing health record, track vitals, connect and communicate with doctors, and support remote consultations. MedAI is built to serve users globally, not only in Nigeria, and this policy is written accordingly.

This policy explains, in plain language, what information MedAI actually collects, why, how it's used, who it's shared with, and the choices and rights available to you. It is based on a direct review of MedAI's current codebase and infrastructure, not a generic template — where something isn't yet built, isn't yet confirmed, or is a known limitation, this policy says so explicitly rather than describing an idealized version of the product.

This policy will be updated as MedAI's features change. It is a product privacy policy, not a certification — it does not claim compliance with any specific law merely by mentioning that law.

3. Information We Collect

Account and identity information

Name, username, and email address; a securely hashed password, or — if you sign in with Google — your Google account identifier and profile picture; your account role (patient, doctor, or admin); and, if you enable it, a two-factor authentication secret and one-time backup codes.

Health profile information (all optional, provided by you)

Date of birth, gender, height, weight, blood group, country, phone number, medical conditions, allergies, medications (free text), lifestyle information (smoking, alcohol, exercise), and an emergency contact name and phone number.

Vitals and health measurements

Heart rate, SpO2, temperature, blood pressure, glucose, and weight readings, which you either type in manually or capture using the in-app camera-based pulse scanner. The camera video itself is processed entirely on your device and is never uploaded to our servers — only the resulting numeric reading (e.g., a heart-rate value) is sent to and stored by MedAI.

Symptom and triage history

When you use Quick Triage, we store the symptoms you typed along with the AI's triage classification, summary, and confidence level, so you (and, where you've chosen to share it, a matched doctor) can refer back to it. You can bulk-delete your triage history from within the app.

AI assistant conversations (Medical, Safe Space, Mental, Physical Health)

Messages you send to MedAI's AI assistants are transmitted to Google Gemini or OpenRouter to generate a response (see Section 7). As currently implemented, MedAI's own servers do not save the content of these conversations — only an aggregate daily usage count per user is stored, not the messages themselves.

Note: this means if you'd like AI-assistant conversations to persist as part of your medical record (distinct from Quick Triage results, which are saved), that is not currently how the product works — worth confirming this is the intended design.

Doctor-patient conversations

If you chat with a doctor through MedAI, we store the conversation, including message text, any images you send (stored directly in our database), delivery/read status, and whether you've chosen to share your fuller profile with that doctor.

Doctor / professional information (doctor accounts only)

Full name, phone number, medical license number, issuing authority, specialty, years of experience, hospital/clinic affiliation, an optional bio, an uploaded copy of your license document (used for manual verification by our team), your workplace location if you add one, your availability status, and your verification status.

Ratings and appointments

Star ratings (1–5) and optional written reviews patients leave for doctors they've had a conversation with; and appointment requests, including scheduled date/time, reason, urgency, status, and any decline reason.

Location information

If you use the Medical Locator's "search near me," your device coordinates (or a place name you type) are sent to OpenStreetMap's Nominatim (for address search) and Overpass (for nearby-facility search) services to find results. MedAI's backend passes this through to those services but does not separately keep a history of your past location searches. A doctor's own workplace coordinates, if they add one, are stored so it can be shown on the map.

Technical information

MedAI does not use cookies. Your browser's local storage holds your session/authentication token (so you stay signed in) and, for doctor accounts, a small display-name value — not for tracking or advertising. Standard web request logs (such as IP address at time of request) are inherent to any hosting provider; MedAI does not currently operate a dedicated analytics or error-monitoring service. [TO BE CONFIRMED: what default request-log retention applies at the Vercel/Render hosting layer].

Payment information

See Section 10 — MedAI's backend never directly receives or stores your card details.

Notifications

In-app notifications (new message, appointment request/accepted/declined, review received, doctor application approved/rejected, payment successful) are generated and stored within the app. These are not emails. The only transactional email MedAI currently sends is the password-reset verification code.

4. Health and Sensitive Personal Data

Much of what's described above — symptoms, conditions, medications, allergies, vitals, triage results, and doctor-patient conversations — is health information that receives enhanced legal protection ("special category" or "sensitive" data) in many jurisdictions. We process it because it is core to what MedAI does: helping you understand symptoms, maintain a health record, and connect with doctors.

We apply protections including secure password hashing, HTTPS in transit, and access restrictions on doctor-patient data (Section 8) — see Section 15 for a full, honest account of what security measures currently exist and what remains [TO BE CONFIRMED] (for example, whether the database itself applies disk-level encryption at rest is a property of our database host that needs confirming, not something MedAI's application code adds on top).

7. AI and Automated Processing

MedAI currently uses two AI providers: Google Gemini (model: gemini-1.5-flash) as the primary provider, and OpenRouter — configured to auto-select from whichever underlying free model is available — as a fallback if Gemini is unavailable. No other AI provider is used in the current production backend.

When you message an AI assistant, the text of your message (and relevant context) is sent to whichever provider responds. As noted in Section 3, MedAI itself does not persist the content of these conversations beyond an aggregate usage counter — Quick Triage is the exception, where the structured result is deliberately saved.

Because OpenRouter auto-routes to a variable underlying model, the specific downstream provider handling any given message — and that provider's own data retention and training practices — can differ from one message to the next. Those practices are governed by Gemini's and OpenRouter's own privacy policies, not MedAI's, and we encourage reviewing them directly for specifics. MedAI does not currently have a human review/moderation process for AI conversations.

AI-generated output, including triage results, is informational and supportive only — it is not a medical diagnosis and does not replace professional medical care. Where triage indicates urgency, MedAI directs you toward seeking professional attention.

Automated decision-making

Quick Triage uses AI to classify the symptoms you type into an urgency/category level, and can suggest connecting you with an available doctor of a matching specialty, or with the Mental Health assistant. This is a form of automated processing that helps route you, but as currently implemented it does not, by itself, deny care or make a binding decision — you choose whether to act on the suggestion, and any resulting doctor conversation is with a human professional. Where applicable law grants a right to request human review of automated processing, you can do so by contacting us; [TO BE CONFIRMED: a dedicated self-service flow for this doesn't currently exist beyond contacting support directly].

8. Doctor and Healthcare Professional Access

A conversation between a specific patient and a specific doctor is created either automatically (when Quick Triage matches a serious symptom to an available doctor of the right specialty and you choose to start a chat) or through the appointment/doctor-directory flow.

A doctor's access to your fuller profile is limited by default: until you explicitly turn on "Share Profile" within that chat, the doctor only sees your basic name information. Once you share it, the doctor can see your profile, vitals, and triage/symptom history — and you can turn sharing back off at any time, which hides that information going forward.

Chat messages themselves remain visible to both parties regardless of profile-sharing status, since a conversation inherently requires that. A doctor never sees MedAI's full patient list — only patients they have an actual conversation with.

Current limitation: there is no way to fully end a conversation or remove a doctor's access to past chat messages once a conversation exists. Turning off "Share Profile" hides your profile, vitals, and triage history going forward, but the message history within that conversation remains visible to both parties — there is no "disconnect from this doctor" feature today. Access to a patient's data by a doctor is also not currently logged in an audit trail.

9. Medical Records and Patient-Controlled Sharing

Triage history and vitals readings accumulate as new records over time rather than overwriting previous entries, functioning as an ongoing personal history. Doctor-patient chat transcripts and any images sent are likewise retained as part of that conversation.

Current limitation: MedAI does not yet have a dedicated, structured place to store "prescriptions," "test results," or general medical documents as their own manageable records. Today, the closest equivalents are the free-text "medications" field on your profile, images exchanged in a doctor chat, and the referral-letter generator — which creates a letter for you to send yourself and does not keep a copy on MedAI's servers. If a real medical-document/prescription/test-result store is intended as part of the product, it has not yet been built, and this policy should be revisited once it is.

The only sharing control that currently exists is the per-doctor "Share Profile" toggle described in Section 8 — there is no more granular control (e.g., sharing vitals but not triage history) today. Records are retained by default until you delete them individually (where that's possible) or delete your account (Section 20) — there is no automatic expiry or archival currently implemented.

10. Payments and Subscriptions

MedAI's current, live payment provider is Bachs (bachs.io). MedAI's backend computes your subscription price itself server-side — it never trusts a client-submitted amount — and redirects you to Bachs' own hosted checkout page. MedAI does not directly handle, see, or store your card number, CVV, or other full card details at any point.

What MedAI does store: a transaction reference, Bachs' checkout/session identifier, the amount and currency, the plan type (monthly/yearly), the payment status, and Bachs' own provider transaction reference once confirmed via a cryptographically verified webhook. Any billing or cardholder details Bachs itself collects are governed by Bachs' own privacy policy. [TO BE CONFIRMED: link to Bachs' current privacy policy to reference here].

15. Data Security

  • Passwords are protected using bcrypt, a secure salted password-hashing algorithm — never stored in plain text or reversible encryption.
  • Two-factor authentication (TOTP-based, compatible with apps like Google Authenticator) is available as an optional account security feature, with one-time backup codes.
  • Data in transit is served over HTTPS via our hosting providers (Vercel and Render). [TO BE CONFIRMED: any additional transport-layer hardening beyond each platform's defaults].
  • Data at rest is stored in a managed PostgreSQL database. Whether our database host applies disk-level encryption at rest is a property of that provider — [TO BE CONFIRMED with database host]. MedAI does not currently apply additional application-level encryption to health-data fields beyond the database's own storage.
  • Access control: authentication is required for all patient/doctor API routes; role-based checks restrict admin-only and doctor-only endpoints; a doctor's access to a specific patient's fuller profile requires that patient's explicit "Share Profile" toggle (Section 8).
  • Security headers are set using the Helmet middleware.
  • Rate limiting applies to login, registration, and password-reset endpoints to reduce brute-force and abuse risk.
  • Administrative access: an internal admin role exists to approve doctor applications and manage users. [TO BE CONFIRMED: how many people hold admin access today and what internal controls govern it].
  • Backups: [TO BE CONFIRMED with hosting/database provider — no custom backup system exists in the application code itself].

MedAI does not currently hold any formal security certification (such as SOC 2 or ISO 27001), and this policy does not claim otherwise.

19. Reviews, Ratings and User-Generated Content

After a patient has had a conversation with a doctor, they may leave a 1–5 star rating and an optional written comment for that doctor. This is limited to one review per patient-doctor pair, and both the rating and comment are visible to other patients viewing that doctor's profile. The average rating and review count are shown on the doctor's card wherever they appear in the app (Medical Locator, appointments, triage doctor-match).

20. Account Deletion

You can permanently delete your account at any time from Settings. This is immediate and irreversible: it removes your account and, because nearly every record in our database is linked to your account and cascades on deletion, it also removes your profile, vitals, triage history, conversations and messages, appointments, reviews, notifications, and payment records tied to that account.

Worth knowing: because deletion cascades to payment records too, this may need reconciling against jurisdictions that require financial/transaction records to be retained for a minimum period regardless of account deletion (Section 14) — Bachs, our payment processor, may separately retain its own copy of transaction records under its own policy even after MedAI's copy is deleted.
